\subsection{Baseline Performance Using LASSO}

We started by evaluating the baseline performance using LASSO for reconstruction. LASSO (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator) assumes sparsity in the signal and solves the following optimization problem:
\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = \arg \min_x \|y - Ax\|_2^2 + \lambda \|x\|_1,
\end{equation}
\\
where \( y \) is the measurement vector, \( A \) is the measurement matrix, and \( \lambda \) is a regularization parameter. This method aims to find a sparse solution \( x \) that approximates the original signal.
\\
From our experiments, we observed that the performance of LASSO is inadequate for this task until a substantial number of measurements (high \( M \)) is available. Even then, the reconstructions are often noisy.
\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{assets/Lasso error per pixel.png}
    \caption{Error per pixel using LASSO for compressed sensing as a function of the compressed dimension \( M \)}
    \label{fig:LASSO performance}
\end{figure}
\\
The figure (6.3) illustrates the reconstructed images as the number of measurements \( M \) increases.
\begin{figure}[hbt!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{assets/lasso reconstructions.png}
    \caption{Reconstructions as a function of the increasing number of measurements \( M \)}
    \label{fig:LASSO reconstructions}
\end{figure}
\\\\
We observe that more than 400 measurements are needed for coherent reconstruction that effectively suppresses background noise, although 100 measurements are sufficient to start discerning the digit under the noise. This is achievable because the underlying shape is very simple. For more sophisticated datasets, achieving similar results would be more challenging.

\subsection{Improved Performance Using Generative Networks}

Generative networks, in both VAE and DCGAN forms, significantly enhance performance, requiring far fewer measurements to achieve the same quality of reconstruction as LASSO. The following example (fig. 6.4) demonstrates the generation of images with a given number of measurements \( M \).
\\\\
We note that the generated number matches the original with as few as 20 measurements.
\\\\
This significant improvement showcases the effectiveness of generative networks in compressed sensing, highlighting their ability to reconstruct high-quality images from minimal data.\\
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{assets/gan_gen_20/sequential generation through epochs with error.png}
    \caption{Sample images generated by a DCGAN with a latent vector of dimension 20}
    \label{fig:DCGAN generation z = 20}
\end{figure}
\hfill \break
Furthermore, we can see how the four models (VAEs with 20 and 30 latent vector dimension and DCGANs with 20 and 30 latent dimension) stack against each other in the error per pixel for every increasing value of M.\\
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{assets/losses.png}
    \caption{Losses per pixel using generative models}
    \label{fig:enter-label}
\end{figure}
\hfill \break
To ensure a fair comparison of the models' behavior, we trained them on the same machine for a similar number of epochs.
\\
From the graph above, we can conclude that all the involved generative networks perform significantly better in terms of loss per pixel with respect to the reconstruction obtained with LASSO (figure 6.2 for reference). Among the four of them, the DCGAN with latent dimension equal to 20 is the one that performs better with few measurements, while as the number of measurements increases we can notice that the models tend to achieve the same performances, settling down on an loss of 0.1 - 0.2 after around 400 measurements.
\\\\
DCGAN is a suitable choice for compressed sensing in a scenario in which high precision and few measurements are required.
\\
On the other hand, taking into consideration the amount of time needed for training, that in the case of DCGAN is almost 11 times higher than VAE, if the slightly better performance of DCGAN is negligible and it is preferred a less time and resources consuming alternative, than VAE proves to be a valid solution.
